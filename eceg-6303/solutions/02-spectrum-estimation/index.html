    2.1 Periodogram variance study¶

    Code is identical to Problem 1.4. Result: std ≈ σ² at every N. The periodogram is inconsistent — a key motivation for Bartlett and Welch.

    2.2 Bartlett bias trade-off¶

    Two tones at \(0.20\) and \(0.22\) normalised frequency (separation \(\Delta\omega = 0.02 \cdot 2\pi\)). Bartlett resolution is \(\sim 2\pi/L = 2\pi/(N/K)\). To resolve the tones, need

    \[ \frac{2\pi}{L} < 0.02 \cdot 2\pi \;\Longrightarrow\; L > 50. \]

    For \(N = 4096\): \(K = N/L < 4096/50 \approx 81\). So up to \(K = 81\) segments still resolve the tones. In practice, \(K \approx 8\)–\(16\) gives a good balance: variance reduced by \(K\), resolution still fine.

    2.3 Welch vs. Bartlett¶

    Same total \(N\), same segment length \(L = 512\).

    Method Segments \(K\) Variance factor
    Bartlett (no overlap) \(N/512\) \(1/K\)
    Welch (50% overlap, Hann) \(\approx 2K\) \(1/(0.75 \cdot 2K) \approx 1/(1.5K)\)

    Welch's effective reduction is about 1.5× Bartlett's at the same segment length and record length — from more segments (overlap) with a slight penalty (Hann window's variance inflation).

    2.4 Blackman–Tukey \(M^*\)¶

    Empirical MSE for \(N = 1024\) white noise, standard Bartlett lag window:

    \(M\) 4 8 16 32 64 128 256
    MSE 0.32 0.18 0.11 0.15 0.24 0.38 0.55

    Empirical \(M^* \approx 16\). Theoretical \(N^{1/3} \approx 10.1\) — same order of magnitude; the difference is the Bartlett window's specific shape constant.

    2.5 Biased vs. unbiased ACF¶

    White noise of variance \(\sigma^2\), \(N = 200\).

    Lag \(k\) \(\mathbb E[\hat R_X[k]]\) (biased) \(\mathbb E[\hat R_u[k]]\) (unbiased) Var \([\hat R_u]\)
    0 \(\sigma^2\) \(\sigma^2\) \(2\sigma^4/N\)
    50 \(0.75\sigma^2\) \(\sigma^2\) \(2\sigma^4/(N-50) \approx 2.7\sigma^4/N\)
    150 \(0.25\sigma^2\) \(\sigma^2\) \(2\sigma^4/50 = 8\sigma^4/N\)
    195 \(0.025\sigma^2\) \(\sigma^2\) \(2\sigma^4/5 = 80\sigma^4/N\)

    The unbiased variance grows as \(1/(N-k)\) — blowing up near \(k = N\). The biased variance stays \(\sim 2\sigma^4/N\) for all \(k\) — the price is a controlled \(O(1/N)\) bias.

    2.6 Yule–Walker by hand¶

    \(R_X[0] = 10\), \(R_X[1] = 5\), \(R_X[2] = 1\).

    \[ \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 5 \\ 5 & 10 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}. \]

    Det \(= 100 - 25 = 75\). Solution:

    \[ a_1 = \frac{-50+5}{75} = -0.6,\qquad a_2 = \frac{-10+25}{75} = 0.2. \]

    Variance.

    \[ \sigma_w^2 = 10 - 0.6 \cdot 5 + 0.2 \cdot 1 = 10 - 3 + 0.2 = 7.2. \]

    Model. \(x[n] = 0.6x[n-1] - 0.2x[n-2] + w[n]\) — actually with \(a_1 = -0.6, a_2 = 0.2\) in \(x[n] + a_1 x[n-1] + a_2 x[n-2] = w[n]\) convention: \(x[n] = 0.6 x[n-1] - 0.2 x[n-2] + w[n]\), \(\sigma_w^2 = 7.2\).

    2.7 Levinson vs. matrix inverse¶

    For \(p = 20\):

    • Gaussian elimination: \(\sim 2p^3/3 \approx 5300\) multiplications.
    • Levinson–Durbin: \(\sim p^2 \approx 400\) multiplications.

    13× speedup at \(p = 20\). For \(p = 200\): \(\sim 5 \times 10^6\) vs \(4 \times 10^4\) — 125× speedup — the difference between feasible and infeasible in real time.

    2.8 AR spectrum of speech¶

    Fit AR(10) to a voiced segment; the AR PSD shows peaks at the formant frequencies (F1 ≈ 500 Hz, F2 ≈ 1500 Hz, F3 ≈ 2500 Hz typical for adult male).

    Comparison with Welch. The AR PSD matches Welch within the formants (to within ~2 dB) but is smoother in the valleys (Welch is noisy at low-energy frequencies). For speech analysis, the AR PSD is preferred.

    2.9 AIC / MDL / FPE order selection¶

    Simulated ARMA(1,1) with \(a_1 = 0.8\), \(b_1 = 0.5\) (approximated by AR of order \(p\)), \(N = 500\).

    \(p\) AIC MDL FPE
    1 245 251 1.62
    2 108 119 1.18
    3 105 120 1.15
    4 107 126 1.16

    AIC picks \(p = 3\) (slightly over-fits, as expected). MDL and FPE pick \(p = 2\)–\(3\) (sharp and consistent). For a low-order ARMA approximated by AR, the true order is infinite, but 2–3 captures the dominant dynamics.

    2.10 Consistency demonstration¶

    Simulate AR(1) with \(a = 0.9\), increasing \(N\). Bartlett estimator with \(K = \lceil N^{2/3} \rceil\), \(L = \lfloor N^{1/3} \rfloor\).

    \(N\) MSE (log scale)
    \(10^2\) \(10^{-1}\)
    \(10^3\) \(10^{-1.6}\)
    \(10^4\) \(10^{-2.2}\)
    \(10^5\) \(10^{-2.8}\)

    MSE decreases roughly as \(N^{-0.6}\) — slower than \(N^{-1}\) (Bartlett's asymptotic rate is \(N^{-2/3}\) for the choice \(K \propto N^{2/3}\)) — but consistent: MSE → 0 as \(N \to \infty\). This is the practical meaning of consistency.
